# Example brief: a website for a local home-services contractor

*A worked example of the 10-section format, written from a real job description. Identifying details are removed, so the original job description is not included.*

## 1. Business problem

The company has no professional online presence. Customers searching online for its services cannot find it, so it loses business. It has no place to show its services and past work, build credibility or capture leads. In a competitive local market, this costs revenue and growth.

## 2. Target users

| User segment | Needs |
|---|---|
| **Homeowners** | Repair, replacement or installation work; a trustworthy local contractor; clear service information |
| **Property managers and commercial building owners** | Commercial services, reliability, examples of similar projects |
| **Comparers** | Comparing contractors, reading reviews, viewing past projects before getting in touch |
| **Existing customers** | Contact details, service information, follow-up support |

## 3. Desired outcome

A professional, responsive website that:

- Establishes credibility and trust with potential customers
- Shows the services and completed projects
- Captures inbound leads through a working contact form
- Is easy for the company to update without technical skills
- Loads quickly on every device

## 4. Key user journeys

1. **Discovery and research.** The user searches for the service → lands on the home page → opens Services → reviews what is offered.
2. **Portfolio evaluation.** The user opens the gallery → views past projects → judges quality and fit.
3. **Lead capture.** The user opens Contact → fills in the form → submits → the company follows up.
4. **Company evaluation.** The user opens About → learns the company's story, team and values → builds trust.
5. **Mobile access.** The user opens the site on a phone → finds the phone number and contact form quickly.

## 5. MVP scope

- Clean, responsive design (mobile, tablet, desktop)
- **Home**: company overview, value proposition, key services
- **About**: company story, team, values, trust signals
- **Services**: residential and commercial; repair, replacement, installation
- **Gallery**: project photos with filtering or categories
- **Contact**: working contact form, phone number, business address, map
- Speed optimization (target: under 3 seconds to load)
- Content the company can update itself (CMS-friendly)
- Basic SEO (meta tags, semantic HTML, sitemap)
- Cross-browser testing
- Domain connection and SSL

## 6. Out of scope

- E-commerce or online payments
- Customer portal or user accounts
- Blog or content marketing
- Third-party booking or scheduling integration
- Multiple languages
- Analytics beyond basic tracking
- Ongoing content updates after handover (beyond initial training)
- Creating or managing social media accounts

## 7. Assumptions

- The company provides branding (logo, colors, high-quality project photos)
- The company provides the written content (service descriptions, About text, contact details)
- The initial build and launch take 1–3 months
- Post-launch support will be provided; its scope and duration are still to be defined
- The company owns or will buy a domain
- Hosting will be arranged by the company or the developer
- One main stakeholder gives feedback and approvals

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The client delivers content late | Timeline slips | Agree content deadlines up front |
| Scope creep during development | Budget and timeline overrun | A clear change-request process; lock the MVP scope |
| An image-heavy gallery slows mobile pages | Poor experience, weaker search ranking | Compress images; lazy-load them |
| Search results take time to appear | Stakeholder disappointment | Set expectations; focus on the technical SEO foundation |
| Depending on third-party hosting or domain setup | Launch delay | Arrange domain and hosting early |
| Support needs exceed the estimate | Strained capacity | Define support boundaries in the contract |

## 9. Discovery questions

1. Which services come first (residential, commercial, emergency repairs, inspections)?
2. Does the company have branding (logo, colors, fonts, project photos)?
3. What traffic and monthly lead volume does it expect?
4. Who will provide and maintain the content?
5. Is there a platform preference (WordPress, static site, custom build)?
6. Does the company already have a domain and hosting, or should the developer arrange them?
7. What does "ongoing support" include: maintenance, content updates, hosting, or all three?
8. What is the budget range?
9. Are there competitor websites the client likes or wants to stand apart from?
10. What is the target launch date?
11. How will success be measured (leads, calls, form submissions, rankings)?

## 10. Definition of Done

- [ ] All five pages (Home, About, Services, Gallery, Contact) are live and working
- [ ] The contact form delivers inquiries to the company's email
- [ ] The site is fully responsive on mobile, tablet and desktop
- [ ] Pages load in under 3 seconds on a standard connection
- [ ] Basic SEO tags and structure are on every page
- [ ] SSL is active and the domain is connected
- [ ] Tested in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge
- [ ] The client is trained to update basic content (text, images, contact details)
- [ ] The client signs off on the final delivery
- [ ] A post-launch support plan is agreed and written down
