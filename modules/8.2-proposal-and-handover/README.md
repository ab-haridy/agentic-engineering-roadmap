# 8.2 · Proposal, milestones and handover

**Phase 8 · Client delivery** · Estimated time: 8–10 hours

## Objective

Manage a complete client engagement, not only the build: the proposal, the scope, the milestones, changes along the way, the demo and the handover.

## Why it matters

Most failed projects don't fail on code. They fail on a vague scope, a change nobody priced, a demo that surprised the client, or a handover that left them unable to run what they paid for. These documents are what make you look, and be, reliable. They are also where agents help a great deal, as long as you own the decisions.

## Learn

- Proposals that lead with the client's outcome
- Measurable scope
- Estimating, and stating your assumptions
- Milestones with acceptance criteria
- Change requests: how new asks get evaluated, priced and approved
- Demos that show progress against the scope
- Client communication
- Handover, and post-launch support

## Practical task

Treat **your Phase 4 product** as a real client engagement, and prepare the complete delivery package. Reuse your brief, requirements and architecture; this is about packaging them for a client.

1. **Proposal:** the client's problem and outcome first, then your approach, the scope, the milestones, the estimate and its assumptions, and what is not included.
2. **Milestones:** 3 to 5, each with deliverables and acceptance criteria the client can check.
3. **Change-request template:** what was asked, its effect on scope, time and cost, and the decision.
4. **Demo script:** a short walkthrough of the main journey, tied to the acceptance criteria. Record one run of it.
5. **Handover:** everything the client needs to run the product: access and accounts, how to deploy, how to operate it day to day, known limitations, and where everything is.
6. **Support plan:** what is covered after launch, response times, and what counts as new work.

**Keep internal material internal.** Your instructions files, skills, prompts and agent logs are your way of working, not the client's product. Leave them out of the handover unless the contract says otherwise.

work/8.2-proposal-and-handover/

## Definition of Done

- [ ] The proposal leads with the client's outcome
- [ ] The scope is measurable, and exclusions are stated
- [ ] The estimate states its assumptions
- [ ] Every milestone has acceptance criteria
- [ ] A change process exists
- [ ] The demo follows the acceptance criteria, and a run is recorded
- [ ] The client could run the product from the handover alone
- [ ] The handover contains no internal agent material unless agreed
